The Food Was Never the Hard Part

If you run a lab, an auditing firm, or a certification body, you already know where the real friction sits in this business. It isn't testing capacity, and it isn't competence — most labs are good at what they do. It's this: you want new food-company customers constantly, and wanting them does not mean knowing who needs you, when they need you, or getting there before another lab does. Nobody sends you a notice saying a snack company is about to need a new certification next month. You just wait, and hope the phone rings before someone else's does.

Meanwhile the food maker on the other side of that gap has the mirror-image problem. They can search "food testing lab" and get forty results back in seconds. That was never hard. What's hard is they have no way to know which of those forty can actually handle their specific product, which one is going to sit on their file for three weeks before replying, or whether the one they picked is even competent for what they need tested. If the packaging is already printed and a launch date is already set, picking wrong doesn't cost a little time. It delays the whole thing while they wait for someone to get back to them.

Choosing the wrong lab can delay an entire launch. Choosing the right one, fast, is the whole service.

Here's what turns a name into a real lead instead of a guess: a certification number. A food company that already paid for and passed one certification has proven two things at once — they need this kind of service, and they're willing to pay for it. If that certification is recent, something just happened inside that business, which usually means another test, another audit, or another renewal is already coming. That's not a cold name pulled from a directory. That's a company already in motion, with money already moving toward exactly what you sell.

You've heard the generic pitches before — more meetings, more leads, some workflow that promises to fill your pipeline. You've said no to those, and you were right to. What actually moves the needle is someone who can tell you, specifically, which food makers are about to need exactly what you offer, and put you in front of them before another lab gets there first.

— Abdullah Shareef, routing between food safety labs and food makers, 2026
